\begin{quote}
\small\textbf{Abstract.}
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are a leading paradigm for energy-efficient inference,
but deterministic robustness guarantees for them remain scarce because the membrane
\emph{reset} couples binary spike decisions across time. The realistic
reset-by-subtraction rule is the central obstacle: a single noise-induced spike flip
alters the post-spike subtraction at the following step, which can flip a further spike,
producing a temporal cascade that the standard per-step margin argument cannot bound. We
introduce a deterministic, per-neuron, per-timestep certificate of spike preservation for
a trained, direct-encoded leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) layer under $\ell_2$-bounded
input perturbation. The certificate replaces the unknown flip sequence with a conservative
risk-flag sequence and solves the resulting self-referential bound as the \emph{least
fixed point} of a monotone operator on the Boolean lattice $\{0,1\}^T$; existence follows
from the Knaster--Tarski theorem and soundness from a causal temporal induction. The bound
is \emph{attack-agnostic} by construction. Across $27$ trained configurations spanning three
datasets, three leak factors, and three horizons, and a six-point perturbation sweep under
both Gaussian and adversarial (FGSM) attacks, the certificate records \emph{zero} soundness
violations at every operating point while certifying $85$--$93\%$ of hidden activations safe
at small radius and degrading gracefully thereafter. We further show the guarantee composes
soundly across depth: in a four-layer convolutional SNN with up to $98.8\%$ clean accuracy,
the certificate records zero violations at every layer. We position the result against
analyses that respectively discard the reset term \citep{ding2024robust} or treat
reset-by-subtraction only for random, non-leaky networks with average-case probabilistic
bounds \citep{araya2025random}.
\vspace{0.7em}
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) compute with sparse, binary, asynchronous events and are the
native model for neuromorphic hardware such as Loihi and SpiNNaker
\citep{maass1997networks,roy2019towards,davies2018loihi}. As SNNs move toward
reliability-sensitive applications, a basic question follows the trajectory already charted
for conventional networks: if the input is perturbed slightly, is the network's computation
preserved? For artificial neural networks (ANNs) this has produced a mature theory of
certified robustness, spanning randomized smoothing \citep{cohen2019certified} and formal
verification via abstract interpretation, bound propagation, and reachability
\citep{gehr2018ai2,zhang2018crown,tran2019star}. For SNNs the corresponding theory is far
less developed, and the reason is specific and structural: the membrane reset.

\paragraph{The difficulty, intuitively.} For a \emph{single} timestep the question is
elementary. If a neuron's membrane sits at distance $m$ from the firing threshold and the
worst-case perturbation can displace it by at most $B$, then $m>B$ implies the spike cannot
flip---one inequality settles it. The difficulty is entirely \emph{temporal}. Under
reset-by-subtraction, a neuron that fires has the threshold subtracted from its membrane, so
whether it fired at $t-1$ changes its membrane at $t$. If a perturbation flips a spike at
some step, the corresponding subtraction is present in one trajectory and absent in the
other, injecting a discrepancy that propagates forward and can flip further spikes. A single
flip can cascade (Figure~\ref{fig:cascade}). The per-step margin argument breaks because the
effective perturbation each neuron experiences at time $t$ depends on \emph{which earlier
spikes flipped}---precisely the quantities one is trying to certify. This self-reference is
the technical heart of the problem, and resolving it deterministically is our contribution.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_cascade.png}
\caption{The core obstacle. A noise-induced flip at $t{=}3$ changes the post-spike
subtraction, so the clean and perturbed trajectories diverge; the discrepancy propagates
forward through the reset coupling. The certified bound at time $t$ must therefore account
for all earlier flips, yielding a self-referential dependency.}
\label{fig:cascade}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The gap.} Two recent analyses address SNN stability but each sidesteps this
cascade. \citet{ding2024robust} derive a membrane-potential perturbation $L_2$ stability
bound and convert it into a training regularizer; however, their derivation adopts the
\emph{reset-to-zero} rule and explicitly discards the reset fluctuation to obtain a smooth
recursion, and their guarantee concerns the aggregate last-layer perturbation rather than
individual spikes. \citet{araya2025random} study noise sensitivity of LIF classifiers
through Boolean (Fourier--Walsh) analysis \citep{odonnell2014analysis} and \emph{do} treat
reset-by-subtraction, but restrict to randomly initialized, non-leaky ($\beta{=}1$) networks
and obtain \emph{average-case probabilistic} bounds. Empirical defenses---adversarial and
crafted-noise training \citep{kundu2021hire,sharmin2020inherent}, input-boundary relaxation
\citep{liang2022toward}, and rate-coding randomized smoothing \citep{mukhoty2024certified}---
improve robustness but provide no deterministic, per-instance guarantee for the realistic
dynamics. None delivers a deterministic, per-instance certificate for a \emph{trained},
\emph{leaky} network under \emph{reset-by-subtraction}. This is the gap we fill.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item A tight per-neuron displacement bound for the non-resetting case, improving on a
global spectral bound by row-norm and partial-sum refinements (\S\ref{sec:clean}).
\item A reset-corrected bound cast as a monotone operator on $\{0,1\}^T$, with existence of a
least fixed point via Knaster--Tarski \citep{knaster1928theoreme,tarski1955lattice} and a
soundness proof by causal temporal induction; an iterative algorithm with $O(NT^2)$ cost per
sample (\S\ref{sec:reset}--\S\ref{sec:algo}).
\item Empirical validation on trained MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 classifiers: zero
soundness violations across $27$ configurations and a six-point $\eps$-sweep under both random
and adversarial attacks, certifying $85$--$93\%$ of activations safe at small radius
(\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\item A demonstration that the certificate \emph{composes soundly across depth}: in a
four-layer convolutional SNN with up to $98.8\%$ clean accuracy, zero violations at every
layer (\S\ref{sec:depth}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Discrete-time LIF neuron.} We use the discrete leaky integrate-and-fire model
\citep{gerstner2002spiking,eshraghian2023training} with direct (constant-current) encoding:
the flattened input $\bm{x}\in\R^d$ is injected at every timestep, so only the membrane
evolves in time. For neuron $i\in[N]$ at timestep $t\in[T]$,
\begin{align}
V(i,t) &= \beta\,V(i,t{-}1) + (W_1\bm{x})_i - \theta\,s(i,t{-}1), \label{eq:lif}\\
s(i,t) &= \ind\!\left[\,V(i,t)\ge\theta\,\right], \qquad V(i,0)=0, \label{eq:spk}
\end{align}
with leak $\beta\in(0,1)$, first-layer weights $W_1\in\R^{N\times d}$ (rows $\bm{w}_i$),
threshold $\theta$ (set to $1$), $N$ neurons, and horizon $T$. The term $-\theta\,s(i,t{-}1)$
is the subtractive reset.

\paragraph{Threat model.} Given a clean input $\bm{x}$, an adversary chooses
$\bm{\delta}\in\R^d$ with $\norm{\bm{\delta}}_2\le\eps$ and presents $\bm{x}'=\bm{x}+\bm{\delta}$.
We write $V'(i,t),\,s'(i,t)$ for the perturbed trajectory,
$\Delta V(i,t)=V'(i,t)-V(i,t)$ for the membrane displacement, and
$e(i,t)=\abs{s'(i,t)-s(i,t)}\in\{0,1\}$ for the absolute spike discrepancy.

\section{The Clean-Case Bound}
\label{sec:clean}

We first analyze the idealized non-resetting recurrence
$V(i,t)=\beta V(i,t{-}1)+(W_1\bm{x})_i$, where a closed-form bound is attainable.

\begin{lemma}[Non-resetting displacement]\label{lem:clean}
Under the non-resetting recurrence, for all $i,t$ and all $\norm{\bm{\delta}}_2\le\eps$,
\begin{equation}
\abs{\Delta V(i,t)} \le \Bin(i,t,\eps):=\eps\,\norm{\bm{w}_i}_2\,\frac{1-\beta^{\,t}}{1-\beta}.
\label{eq:Binput}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unrolling from $t$ to $1$ with $V(i,0)=0$ gives
$V(i,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{t-k}(W_1\bm{x})_i$, and identically for $\bm{x}'$, so
$\Delta V(i,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{t-k}(W_1\bm{\delta})_i$. Since $(W_1\bm{\delta})_i$ is
independent of $k$, $\abs{\Delta V(i,t)}=\abs{(W_1\bm{\delta})_i}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{t-k}$.
By Cauchy--Schwarz, $\abs{(W_1\bm{\delta})_i}\le\norm{\bm{w}_i}_2\eps$, and
$\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{t-k}=(1-\beta^t)/(1-\beta)$. Multiplying gives \eqref{eq:Binput}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Tightness]\label{rem:tight}
Bound~\eqref{eq:Binput} improves on the global spectral bound
$\eps\,\norm{W_1}_2/(1-\beta)$ on both factors: $\norm{\bm{w}_i}_2\le\norm{W_1}_2$, and the
partial sum is strictly below $1/(1-\beta)$ for finite $t$. The Cauchy--Schwarz step is tight
in the worst case ($\bm{\delta}\parallel\bm{w}_i$), so \eqref{eq:Binput} is the tightest
row-wise linear bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Margin and flag]\label{def:flag}
The margin is $m(i,t)=\abs{V(i,t)-\theta}$, and the flag is
$\tau(i,t)=\ind[m(i,t)<B(i,t,\eps)]$, with $\tau=1$ ``at risk'' and $\tau=0$ ``certified safe''.
\end{definition}

\section{The Reset Obstacle and the Fixed Point}
\label{sec:reset}

Retaining the reset term and subtracting the two instances of \eqref{eq:lif},
$\Delta V(i,t)=\beta\Delta V(i,t{-}1)+(W_1\bm{\delta})_i-\theta(s'-s)(i,t{-}1)$. Unrolling and
applying the triangle inequality, using $\abs{(s'-s)(i,k{-}1)}=e(i,k{-}1)$, gives the exact
reset bound
\begin{equation}
\abs{\Delta V(i,t)}\le \Bin(i,t,\eps)+\theta\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{\,t-k}\,e(i,k{-}1).
\label{eq:reset-bound}
\end{equation}
The second term contains the unknown flips $e$, the very quantities the perturbation alters.
We replace $e(i,\cdot)$ by a risk-flag vector $\bm{\tau}_i\in\{0,1\}^T$ and define the
component-wise operator $\F:\{0,1\}^T\!\to\{0,1\}^T$,
\begin{equation}
\F(\bm{\tau}_i)_t=\ind\!\Big[\,m(i,t)\le
\underbrace{\Bin(i,t,\eps)+\theta\!\sum_{k=1}^t\!\beta^{t-k}\tau(i,k{-}1)}_{=:\ \Brst(\bm{\tau})_t}\Big].
\label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
The non-strict ``$\le$'' is deliberate; it is the hinge of the soundness proof.

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity]\label{lem:mono}
$\bm{\tau}_A\le\bm{\tau}_B\Rightarrow\F(\bm{\tau}_A)\le\F(\bm{\tau}_B)$ under the
component-wise order on $\{0,1\}^T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every coefficient $\theta\beta^{t-k}\ge0$, so $\Brst(\bm{\tau})_t$ is non-decreasing in each
$\tau(i,k{-}1)$; the indicator $u\mapsto\ind[m(i,t)\le u]$ is non-decreasing in $u$; the
composition is order-preserving.
\end{proof}

Since $(\{0,1\}^T,\le)$ is a finite complete lattice, Knaster--Tarski \citep{tarski1955lattice}
guarantees a \emph{least} fixed point $\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min}$, obtained by the upward Kleene
iteration $\bm{\tau}^{(0)}=\bm0,\ \bm{\tau}^{(n+1)}=\F(\bm{\tau}^{(n)})$, which converges in at
most $T$ steps. Among all fixed points it flags the fewest coordinates, hence certifies the
most activations safe.

\section{Soundness}
\label{sec:sound}

\begin{theorem}[Least fixed-point soundness]\label{thm:sound}
For any fixed point $\bm{\tau}^{*}$ of $\F$, $e(i,t)\le\tau^{*}(i,t)$ for all $t$.
Consequently $\tau^{*}(i,t)=0\Rightarrow e(i,t)=0$: no certified-safe activation can flip, and
the number of soundness violations is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on $t$; well posed because $\Brst(\bm{\tau}^{*})_t$ depends only on flags at $k<t$
(causality). \emph{Base ($t=0$):} $\Delta V(i,0)=0$, so $e(i,0)=0\le\tau^{*}(i,0)$.
\emph{Step:} assume $e(i,k)\le\tau^{*}(i,k)$ for $k<t$. Substituting into
\eqref{eq:reset-bound} and using non-negativity of coefficients,
$\abs{\Delta V(i,t)}\le \Brst(\bm{\tau}^{*})_t$. If $\tau^{*}(i,t)=0$, then since
$\bm{\tau}^{*}=\F(\bm{\tau}^{*})$ and \eqref{eq:F} is non-strict, $\tau^{*}(i,t)=0$ is the
strict negation $m(i,t)>\Brst(\bm{\tau}^{*})_t$. Chaining,
$\abs{\Delta V(i,t)}\le\Brst(\bm{\tau}^{*})_t<m(i,t)$, so $V'(i,t)$ cannot cross $\theta$ and
$s'(i,t)=s(i,t)$, i.e.\ $e(i,t)=0\le\tau^{*}(i,t)$. If $\tau^{*}(i,t)=1$ the bound is
immediate. The induction closes; specialize to $\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Attack-agnosticism]\label{rem:agnostic}
$\Bin$ depends only on $(\eps,\norm{\bm{w}_i}_2,\beta,\theta,T)$ and not on the direction of
$\bm{\delta}$. The certified-safe set is therefore identical for every $\ell_2$-bounded
perturbation, the worst case included; the certificate need not be recomputed per attack.
\end{remark}

\section{Algorithm and Complexity}
\label{sec:algo}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:lfp} computes $\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min}$ by upward Kleene iteration. The inner
reset-term sum dominates; maintaining the discounted partial sums incrementally and using that
each coordinate flips at most once yields a per-sample cost of $O(NT^2)$ and memory $O(NT)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Least Fixed-Point Spike-Preservation Certificate}
\label{alg:lfp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require clean run $V(i,t)$, weights $W_1$, $\beta,\theta,\eps,T$
\State $m(i,t)\gets\abs{V(i,t)-\theta}$;\quad
       $\Bin(i,t)\gets\eps\norm{\bm{w}_i}_2(1-\beta^t)/(1-\beta)$
\State $\bm{\tau}\gets\bm{0}$
\Repeat
  \For{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \State $r_t\gets\theta\sum_{k=1}^{t}\beta^{t-k}\tau(i,k{-}1)$
    \State $\tau_{\mathrm{new}}(i,t)\gets\ind[m(i,t)\le \Bin(i,t)+r_t]$
  \EndFor
\Until{$\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{new}}=\bm{\tau}$}\quad($\le T$ iterations)
\State \Return $\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min}\gets\bm{\tau}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.} A one-hidden-layer LIF classifier ($N{=}128$, reset-by-subtraction, direct
encoding) with a linear readout on time-averaged spike counts, trained with Adam
($10^{-3}$, $3$ epochs) and a triangular surrogate gradient
\citep{neftci2019surrogate,wu2018spatio}. Perturbations are scaled to exact radius
$\norm{\bm{\delta}}_2=\eps$: Gaussian, and FGSM \citep{goodfellow2015explaining}. We report
\textbf{viol} (pairs with $\tau^{*}_{\min}{=}0$ yet $e{=}1$; must be $0$) and the
\textbf{certified-safe fraction} $\operatorname{mean}(1-\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min})$, over $50$ test
samples per configuration.

\paragraph{Soundness on the $(\beta,T)$ grid.} At $\eps{=}0.1$, across all $27$ configurations
($\beta\in\{0.8,0.9,0.99\}$, $T\in\{10,20,50\}$, three datasets), the certificate registers
\emph{zero} violations and certifies $85$--$93\%$ of activations safe, only weakly dependent on
$\beta$ and $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:grid}).

\section{Compositional Soundness Across Depth}
\label{sec:depth}

The certificate is defined per spiking layer; we now show it \emph{composes soundly} through a
deep network. We train a four-layer convolutional SNN (three Conv-LIF layers and one FC-LIF
layer, reset-by-subtraction throughout, batch-normalized, with a rate readout) to high accuracy
($98.8\%$ MNIST, $92.0\%$ Fashion-MNIST, $70.0\%$ CIFAR-10 at $T{=}8$). The certificate is
applied layer by layer: layer $1$ uses the input radius $\eps$; each subsequent layer uses the
certified upstream flags to form its input bound, with batch-norm folded into the effective
convolution weights. We evaluate $\mathrm{viol}$ and the certified-safe fraction at every layer
on $300$ test samples at $\eps{=}0.1$.

\textbf{Soundness composes.} The decisive observation is that $\mathrm{viol}=0$ at every layer
for all three trained datasets (Table~\ref{tab:depth}, Figure~\ref{fig:depth}). The per-spike
guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} is therefore preserved through composition: at no layer,
on any of the $300$ samples, did the certificate declare a spike safe that subsequently flipped.
This validates the propagation construction as a sound primitive for deep spiking verification.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{SNN robustness.} Empirical work shows SNNs possess partial inherent robustness
depending on encoding and neuronal nonlinearity \citep{sharmin2020inherent}, improvable under
crafted-noise or adversarial training \citep{kundu2021hire}. \citet{liang2022toward} relax the
spike nonlinearity to bound spike-input ranges, and \citet{mukhoty2024certified} transfer
randomized smoothing \citep{cohen2019certified} to rate-encoded SNNs for probabilistic
certificates. These are complementary to our deterministic, per-instance guarantee.

\paragraph{Stability analyses.} \citet{ding2024robust} analyze membrane-potential perturbation
under reset-to-zero, discarding the reset term. \citet{araya2025random} apply Boolean function
analysis \citep{odonnell2014analysis} to obtain average-case noise-sensitivity bounds for
random, non-leaky networks under reset-by-subtraction. \citet{moser2024if} study the
integrate-and-fire neuron as a quantizer and identify the same reset term as the obstacle from
a signal-processing vantage. Table~\ref{tab:pos} situates our contribution.

\paragraph{ANN verification.} Deterministic certification for ANNs spans abstract interpretation
\citep{gehr2018ai2}, bound propagation \citep{zhang2018crown}, and star-set reachability
\citep{tran2019star}. Our operator-on-a-lattice formulation is in the same deterministic spirit,
specialized to the temporal reset coupling unique to SNNs.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, Future Work}
\label{sec:disc}

The central hypothesis---that the reset-induced flip cascade can be bounded deterministically by
a self-referential risk-flag fixed point---is supported: $\bm{\tau}^{*}_{\min}$ is provably sound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}) and empirically unviolated across all single-layer operating points
including under adversarial attack, while remaining non-vacuous (certifying the majority of
activations at realistic radii). The guarantee further composes soundly through a four-layer
network (\S\ref{sec:depth}).

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.} The certificate is on individual spikes; lifting it to a
non-vacuous \emph{output-level} guarantee at depth requires tighter propagation, as the certified
fraction reduces with depth (Table~\ref{tab:depth}) under the worst-case row-norm step
(Remark~\ref{rem:tight}). Sharper propagation---e.g.\ symbolic bound propagation in the spirit of
\citet{zhang2018crown}, adapted soundly to the reset coupling---is the natural next step and the
subject of ongoing work. A complementary direction is the reset geometry: \citet{moser2024if}
show reset-to-mod admits a clean quantization bound, which may inform a tighter per-layer
primitive.

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a deterministic, per-neuron certificate of spike preservation for trained LIF
networks under reset-by-subtraction, formulated as the least fixed point of a monotone causal
operator with existence by Knaster--Tarski and soundness by temporal induction. The certificate
is attack-agnostic, and across $27$ single-layer configurations and a six-point $\eps$-sweep under
both random and adversarial perturbations it produced zero violations while certifying the
majority of activations safe at realistic radii; it further composes soundly across a four-layer
convolutional network. The reset term that has obstructed deterministic SNN robustness analysis
is handled not by discarding it nor by retreating to average-case bounds, but by absorbing it
into a sound, computable fixed point on trained networks.
